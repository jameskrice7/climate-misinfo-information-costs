Dear Editors,

Please consider my manuscript, ``Climate misinformation and quoted liquidity in energy equities,'' for publication as a research article in Energy Economics.

The paper asks whether sector-relevant climate misinformation is associated with the quoted trading costs of listed energy securities. It combines a fixed classification of 217,954 English-language Facebook climate posts with daily financial observations across fossil-fuel and renewable-energy sub-industries. Its contribution is a reproducible assessment of how the liquidity association depends on the comparison used to identify it.

A positive association under monthly controls changes sign after local market-by-date and sector-by-month effects are included on a common sample. The principal specification, with prior-session financial controls, does not establish a positive spread association. The paper reports the economic scale and uncertainty of that estimate, inference that respects sector-level content assignment, and timing, outcome, attention, and leave-out diagnostics. It distinguishes quoted liquidity from transaction price impact and model-assessed content from actual investor exposure.

I believe this question fits the journal's interest in energy finance and the information conditions surrounding energy markets. The results delimit the financial interpretation of climate-content measures without claiming that misinformation is harmless or that the observational estimates identify a causal effect. The accompanying replication materials reproduce every regression table and data figure in the submission from the documented fixed inputs.

The submission package includes the manuscript, supplementary material, separate title page, highlights, and competing-interest declaration. Funding, ethics, data availability, and the use of AI-assisted tools are disclosed in the appropriate documents.
