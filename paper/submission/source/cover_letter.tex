Dear Editors,

Please consider my manuscript, ``The Information Costs of Climate Misinformation: Evidence from Global Financial Data,'' for publication as a research article in Energy Economics.

The paper connects the political production of climate misinformation to the quoted trading conditions of listed energy securities. It combines classifications of 217,954 English-language Facebook climate posts with daily financial observations across nine fossil-fuel and renewable-energy sub-industries, bringing the political-information and market-microstructure literatures into a common empirical framework.

Greater sector-specific exposure is positively associated with quoted spreads under monthly and local market-day controls. The local market-day estimate is statistically discernible under sector/date clustering, showing that the positive association remains after shared local daily conditions are absorbed. Its boundary is explicit: sector-month controls change the sign and leave an imprecise coefficient, including with prior-session controls. The paper reports those findings together, alongside timing, outcome, attention and leave-out checks, without claiming a causal cost estimate or concealing the null results.

I believe this question fits the journal's interest in energy finance and the information conditions surrounding energy markets. The paper offers a sector-sensitive content measure and a substantive account of where the positive liquidity association is observed. The replication materials provide the completed financial estimates, analysis code and editable manuscript sources; access-controlled platform records are required to regenerate the supplied corpus engagement and network summaries.

The submission package includes the manuscript, supplementary material, separate title page, highlights, and competing-interest declaration. Funding, ethics, data availability, and the use of AI-assisted tools are disclosed in the appropriate documents.
